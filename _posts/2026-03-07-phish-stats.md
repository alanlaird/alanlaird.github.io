I've been going to Phish shows since 1995 and I finally sat down and counted them all up. The answer is 72 — a number that feels both "wow, that's a lot" and "is that it?" depending on who you're asking. My setlist file and some help from [ihoz.com](http://www.ihoz.com/PhishStats.html) did most of the work.

## The tools

[**Phish.net**](https://phish.net) is the essential reference — a comprehensive, fan-run setlist database going back to Phish's earliest shows, maintained by volunteers under the [Mockingbird Foundation](https://mbird.org). Every show, every song, every tease, every guest appearance. If you want to know what Phish played on any given night, phish.net is the answer.

[**ihoz.com**](http://www.ihoz.com/PhishStats.html), run by David "ZZYZX" Steinberg, is what you use once you've built up a show history and want to know what it all means. Feed it a text file of dates (one per line) and it cross-references your list against the full Phish setlist database to generate a surprisingly deep statistical breakdown: songs you've seen the most, songs you've never heard live, openers and closers, gap stats, percentage of the catalog covered, songs played in consecutive shows you attended — the works. It's been running since 1997 and is genuinely one of the great pieces of fan-built infrastructure in the Phish world.

## Special events

[![Big Cypress aerial](/img/me/cypress.png)](https://phish.net)

**Big Cypress, FL — December 30–31, 1999.** The millennium run. Phish played all night into New Year's morning on a Seminole reservation in the Florida Everglades — a seven-hour set that included Tweezer → Mango Song → Tweezer at sunrise. Nothing else was quite like it.

**IT Festival — Limestone, ME, August 2–3, 2003.** A former Air Force base in rural Maine. Two days, four sets, and the kind of communal weirdness that only happens at Phish festivals.

**Coventry — Newport State Airport, VT, August 14–15, 2004.** The last shows before the hiatus. The traffic was a disaster — people abandoned their cars on the highway and walked miles in the rain. The shows themselves were raw and emotional. Phish knew it was the end, and you could hear it.

**Festival 8 — Empire Polo Club, Indio, CA, October 30–November 1, 2009.** The reunion Halloween festival. Three nights in the desert with the costume concert on Halloween night. Three years after Coventry it felt miraculous that they were back at all, let alone playing this well.

**Halloween 2016 — MGM Grand Garden Arena, Las Vegas, NV.** Phish unveiled "Kasvot Vaxt," a fictional Scandinavian band whose entire album *í rokk* they'd secretly written and recorded. Nobody in the audience knew it was coming. A perfect Halloween.

## What ihoz would tell me

The stats above are just the venue/date layer — the part I can compute from a text file of dates. What [ihoz.com](http://www.ihoz.com/fromfile.html) actually generates is the *song* layer: which songs I've seen live, how many times, the last time each was played before I saw it, what percentage of the catalog I've heard, what I've somehow missed in 72 shows.

I know from experience that I've seen Tweezer a lot, I've probably never seen Harpua, and there are almost certainly songs I've seen once that I couldn't name. That's the ihoz report I'd actually want to read.

If you have a show history and haven't run it through ihoz, go do it. One date per line, paste it in, and let ZZYZX's 30-year labor of love tell you things about yourself you didn't know you wanted to know.
